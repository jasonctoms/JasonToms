---
title: Cognitive dissonance and tech
---

So, I decided to make my own blog.  I have been wanting a place to put my thoughts,
but don't want any of the big tech companies to control or own my content, and I don't
want it to be at the mercy of some algorithm.  If you found this blog because I linked to it on one
of those sites, I definitely understand the hypocrisy.  And you are reading it on a browser
made by one of those companies, it is hosted on Cloudflare servers, the code for this website
lives on Microsoft's servers, and the framework it is written in is owned by Google and Jetbrains.
Not sure if Jetbrains falls into the same category, but that is kind of the point of this post.

### Tech is cool!
I have the stereotypical engineer's background.  When I was younger I was always taking things apart
and putting them back together, loved LEGO and K'Nex, played Roller Coaster Tycoon, and wanted to
build roller coasters as my career.  So I was always a builder.  And eventually that led to building
computers and the rabbit hole that opens; a rabbit hole I would argue I am still in today.

I still follow tech blogs to keep up to date with new gadgets, and I still want a robot as a pet 
one day.  I was really hoping that was where technology was leading us 😅  I even still feel some
excitement watching new phone announcements, though that excitement is definitely diminishing over
time since I can only care so much about how fast the newest black rectangle is.  I guess the
excitement there is hoping for something new.  And we are starting to get new form factors, but
I just don't think foldables are the end goal, just a stepping stone.  Where are we going?  All
transparent glass, holograms, direct brain interfaces?  I could go on and on but this line of
thinking is what really triggers what I am really trying to talk about today.

And as a software engineer, of course I am excited about LLM technology and all of the changes that
is bringing to how we interact with our stuff.  I am not a Luddite, even if many people would 
misconstrue my opinions to make me seem that way.  What is happening in that space (I refuse to call
it AI since it is not intelligent) is fundamentally changing our relationship with technology, and
I do believe it will be eventually be for the better.  But I do _not_ think it will be for the better
if we just let things continue on the current path because...

### Tech companies are _NOT_ cool
Here is where I might start sounding preachy and where I will lose some people, but the tech
monopolies we find ourselves becoming evermore dependent on are sucking the joy out of technological
advancement.  People will say that all of the improvements we see, all of the new tools we have, are
democratizing tech skills.  But I do not think that could be further from the truth.  "Anyone can 
be a developer now!"  A statement that _could_ be true if hardware prices were reasonable and focus
was on local, ethically trained models.  But in reality, 99% of those "new developers" know nothing
about engineering software, they just know how to iterate by burning through cloud tokens that are 
currently being handed out like cocaine to the masses.

It doesn't take an economist to see [the problem](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy).
All of the "AI" companies are just investing in each other to artificially inflate their value, while
costs are skyrocketing, data centers are being built without any thought of sustainability, and this
tech is being shoved _everywhere_.  The entire economy just being consolidated more and more into
the pockets of private equity and millionaires/billionaires.  Does that sound like democracy to you?  
To me, it sounds like the end of democracy.  I won't turn this post into a rant about the evils of 
late-stage capitalism, because we are all living it.  If you can't see the problems, you are being
willfully ignorant, and it is only a matter of time before you begin to suffer too.

The tech companies aren't even trying to hide it anymore.  Sam Altman has explicitly said:
> We see a future where intelligence is a utility, like electricity or water, and people buy it from 
> us on a meter.

So in his own words -- in his perfect future -- he is going to charge you to think.  So if we become
reliant on these companies (which they are trying so hard to make happen) we will find ourselves even 
deeper into a technofuedalist future than we ever could have realized just a few years ago.  So 
where does that leave us?

### The dissonance
This is reality.  Some of the coolest things from sci-fi are either already here or within our
reach.  But are they going to be for all of us?  Or are we just helping the rich get richer at our
own expense?

If you find yourself becoming too reliant on tech, take a break and challenge yourself to not use it.
If you find yourself doomscrolling just to get one more tiny dopamine hit, put down the phone and
go for a walk; identify some plants or birds or something.  And just talk to people about this.  We
are all going through it together, some are just more aware of it than others.  If we could all
acknowledge that things are not moving in the right direction, maybe we can change it.

At the end of the day, we still need jobs, and being miserable all the time is, well...miserable.
So we have to accept some of the cognitive dissonance.  Savor that moment when you click build and
it deploys.  Relish that review you get where somebody thanks you for your work.  Pat yourself on
the back when you track down that bug you probably made.  And most importantly, celebrate the human
moments you hopefully still have in your day.

I am still going to let myself get excited about tech with the hope that we can make it wholesome
again.  But I am not going to just sit around and wait for somebody else to fix it.  I will host my 
own blog to get my opinions out there and find other small forms of protest.  My favorite sci-fi trope
is "transcendence," where physical beings move on to a higher plane of existence.  We just really 
need to make sure that higher plane is not on Meta's servers.  Could you image the ads?
