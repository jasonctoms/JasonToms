title: Moral corruption and the loss of humanity

A lofty title to try to summarize what I want to write about today...I have honestly been trying
to write this for a couple of weeks now, but it keeps making me grumpy, so I would give up. But
I need to power through, and hopefully it isn't too ranty.

### Moral corruption
I can't figure out exactly when it happened, but sometime in recent years, the
average person in society has lost their moral compass. You can probably see it too,
if you look. Points of view that used to be considered socially unacceptable are now being touted
as "one side" of an issue. I feel like we used to have a pretty decent grasp of right and wrong
as a society. We weren't at a point where there was true equality yet, but it at least seemed like
we were on the right track. Stella is always talking about how she thought her big fight
in her adult years would be about animal rights, and it really seemed like we were getting close
to figuring out the human rights stuff. But here we are now...all the Rage Against the Machine
songs from the nineties are still just as relevant as ever, and it is really depressing.

That statement alone makes my perspective a bit contradictory; if people were writing songs about
hatred and hypocrisy, then it must have been around. And I can admit that part of it is me looking
at the past through a lens of my younger innocence. There has always been horrible stuff going on,
but I am confident we at least went through an era where the average person was _decent_.

These days you have news anchors making fun of rape victims, racists getting streets named after
them, and compulsive liars being idolized to the point of new religions being created (or existing
ones being distorted, but I won't go there in this post...) And since our media and feeds and
politics are filled with these "leaders", the moral zeitgeist has shifted, and it is difficult
to even recognize a lot of people as human anymore.

### Humanity
Biologically we are, of course, animals. The opening sequence of 2001: A Space Odyssey shows the
transition pretty concisely: the apes are always killing each other, then one day, they learn
to kill each other _with tools_. Those tools and what they led to are what set the homo genus
apart from the other apes. But that isn't what made us _human_.

What I consider to be _humanity_ is the social structure of our species. Or potential social
structure, I guess. Being at the top of the food chain, not having any natural predators, and
having the ability to considerably reshape our environment are some of the defining characteristics
of homo sapiens. Apes with tools. But community, altruism, and empathy are the defining
characteristics that make us _human_. Our animal side wants to be above everything; to dominate.
But our human side wants to be a part of it; synergy. Since the dawn of civilization we have been
in the struggle between these two concepts, both intra and interpersonally.

This is actually the foundation of my own moral compass. I truly believe that we as a species
have the ability to transcend the animal parts of ourselves and accept our existence as a part of
the universe. We don't need to dominate and win, we need to thrive, coexist, and understand. I kinda
hate how new-agey that sounds, but I think it makes sense, and I think if that was the shared goal
that we could get past whatever is going on in the world right now.

### My moral foundation
At this point it might be useful to distill my moral framework down into a few bullet points
so it is easier to explain my beliefs. And when I call this a foundation I mean it is DEEP. It
would take monumental effort to change these significantly, especially since I find them pretty
logically sound and objectively morally good.

- We are all the same species and should therefore be considered equal as a starting point.
  - This means judge by actions and not by beliefs; good people do good things.
- Since we are the stewards of our little slice of the universe, it is our responsibility to take care of our environment. This includes other living species as well as our physical environment.
- Treat others the way you want to be treated (I stole this one.)

Obviously there is more to it than these three points but these really are the basis for every
opinion I have or every thought I act on. These are the rules that I won't break, and I don't
really think there is much nuance to be found in them. The nuance comes when you need to apply
these rules to real world situations, but these values don't have to bend.

I really think having a moral core like this might be what a lot of people are missing!

### How can we be better?
Most importantly, have a moral framework that is _yours_. It isn't so important where you get it
from, but you need to own it. If you feel like your moral compass belongs to you, you are much less
susceptible to being manipulated at that level.

But in order to not be manipulated, you also need to be mindful enough to recognize when that is
happening. If you are doomscrolling and some influencer makes you feel something, ask yourself
why you are feeling that, and what their motivation to make you feel that might be. I am self-aware
enough to see that is exactly what I am doing right now, so this is a perfect time to practice! If
some talking head or even so-called politicians are telling you that your neighbor or another member
of your community is "the enemy", try and figure out if that is really true.

And lastly, please, for all of our sakes, learn to fact-check. I have so many conversations with
people who just repeat talking points without a thought. I think a lot of people don't realize that
politicians don't have to tell the truth (and usually don't) and that the news is allowed to lie (and
usually does, if they are a for-profit news network). It doesn't take a lot of research to know that
kids are not going to school one gender and coming home another, or that all immigrants are trying
to scam or harm you. I should probably make a whole post about how to fact-check...but for now I
will end this with the most important piece of advice I can give to try and turn the world around:

## Be a good person!

I really want us to find our humanity again.
