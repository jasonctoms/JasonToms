---
title: Technological advancement alone is not progress
---

I guess I had been feeling too mentally healthy the past couple of weeks because I found myself
scrolling through my LinkedIn feed. This exercise inevitably just makes me angry and cynical, and
of course I know that is the point. Dissent drives engagement, and engagement sells ads. But that
just got me thinking. Obviously my feed is mostly people talking about tech because of the field
I work in, and if you are talking about tech you are talking about AI. And I realized that just
like most of the societal issues today, it seems like people want to split themselves into two 
sides: in this case, pro-AI and anti-AI.

That type of polarity makes having opinions _easy_. But I think a lot of people, myself included,
tend to use this crutch and it causes us to entirely miss the plot. Technology does not exist in a
vacuum; it exists as a piece of our society, pushing and pulling, facilitating and hindering. And it
seems like the average person wants to discuss things out of context. Or at least within a very 
selective context. 

You could see this as a scientific way of thinking: get rid of variables so you can focus on a 
single thing. Which in a scientific or research context is the correct thing to do. But big opinions
on social media rarely follow the rigor of the rest of the scientific method, so removing technology
from its human context creates discussion that sounds productive on the surface, but look any deeper
and a lot of it falls apart.

To add to the discourse, I want to try to explain why both "sides" are missing the point and how 
the tech bros can be right without society collapsing...as long as we can see the big picture.

### What if the tech bros are right?
Probably the strongest and most common argument the pro-AI side makes is "this technology isn't 
going anywhere, so you might as well use it." The way it is usually worded is some combination of
confrontational and defeatist like that, but the core of that argument is very difficult to argue 
against. Technological progress is generally not erased, and I think it is pretty naive to think
this whole can of worms can be closed again. But that doesn't mean that the current trajectory we
are on is the only way forward. _That_ is extremely short-sighted and closed-minded. This is very
early-days tech and We (collectively, all of us) still have a crazy amount of power to determine
how the tech advances and how it is applied.

Going back to the title: technological advancement alone is not progress. It seems very common to
fall into this trap of thinking that any advancement is progress. To avoid arguing semantics, the 
difference I am pointing out between these two words is the micro scale vs the macro scale. Being able 
to crunch more tokens faster is surely advancement in that specific technology, but does that really
push us forward as a society? Is there another type of advancement we could have that would be
better? What are the consequences of continuing down this same path? These are the questions that
need to be asked and addressed. Nothing so simple as "AI good" or "AI bad". Fire can burn and
destroy things, but it can also heat homes and unlock nutrients in food. 

### The missed plot
Before you dismiss this as a thinly veiled pro-AI post, let me be clear that I do not think the tech
bros are right and almost all of the pro-AI arguments I see are harmful and take up space that needs
to be given to the right kinds of discussions. The LinkedIn algorithm is obviously skewed towards
business and not indicative of society as a whole, but it should be representative of the forces
controlling the trajectory of technology. Most of the posts tout speed, efficiency, productivity.
Which may be true, but what is the cost? Being able to produce more garbage faster does not improve
anything (and in light of current events this week might prevent you from traveling, or logging in
to your bank, or checking your email, etc...). And look at the taglines of the people making these
posts. How many of them have "private equity" or "founder" or "CEO" in their tagline?

Everything these days is a grift, and the way AI is used and sold just makes grifting easier than
ever. And at the same time, so many people feel like they _need_ to grift just to get by. Everyone
is trying to make as much money as possible, and most people fail and either have to move on to
their next scam or fall for somebody else's. Everybody just wants to think about the shareholders,
but guess what? The shareholders don't give a shit about you.

AI _cannot_ exist sustainably in the society we have now. So all of these arguments about whether
AI is good or not are totally missing the point. It isn't about AI; it is about the society we live
in and the people running it. Technology does not exist in a vacuum, and if you are trying to see it
that way, your vacuum just ran over wet cat puke and we might want to take a break to clean up
before we keep moving forward. (I would like to see an LLM misuse multiple definitions like that;
that metaphor sucks.)

### Society has to change with tech
For several years now, those of us living outside of algorithmic feeds have been saying the reckless
application of AI literally _everywhere_ by people who know very little would eventually cause
chaos. Elon Musk, in his infinite wisdom and maturity, took a chainsaw to the systems that were
keeping things running. His reasons for doing so are debatable, but on the surface it seems that he
thinks Grok is good enough to replace humans. I only use our dear Elon as an example because he is
the perfect embodiment of everything your standard-model tech bro desires to be. What an abhorrent
human being to be a role model for people driving technology...but I digress.

This idea of "replacing people with machines" is not new. Printing presses replaced scribes, textile
machines replaced a whole range of textile workers, and computers made countless professions
obsolete. Even if current-day LLM technology is nowhere near as good as its overlords would like you
to believe, it is probably already good enough to replace a lot of jobs. But I don't actually see
that as a bad thing, _in theory_.

The 40-hour workweek is something we take for granted as just part of how things are, but in
reality, it is a pretty modern creation. The [eight-hour day movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day_movement)
was a response to industrialization and the businesses trying to take advantage of the new gains in
productivity. Sound familiar? But how many companies are talking about working less to make up for
these productivity gains, or working hard to find new jobs for people who no longer have
one?

If we want to take this to the extreme, my personal opinion is that people shouldn't have to
work, and applying technology the right way could help us achieve this.

### Zero-sum games and the scary S word
Socialism. A word that should not be scary, but, at the risk of going too New York Times with my
opinion, "both sides" make it hard to swallow. Hitler had a big hand in the corruption of the word
by wrapping up full-on fascism and calling it socialism, and it hasn't really recovered since. And
then these days when somebody wants to talk about it, they tend to either want to quote Marx and
whine about specific frameworks and implementations, or use the fascist interpretation. But the root
of the word is "social" as in "society". This is what we need the word to mean again, and it already
does in some contexts. Social security is a perfect example. The _meaning_ of the word is a society
working together to create equality.

One of the biggest misrepresentations of reality people use to describe society is describing it
as a zero-sum game. This assumes everything is finite, and giving something to somebody requires
taking it from somebody else. But that isn't how things work at all. Is something being taken from
somebody to have kids go to public school? Or to have public roads to drive on? Technically, taxes
(which the richest don't pay anyway), but do you feel like kids are stealing from you to get an
education?

Money isn't real, it is just a relatively globally agreed-upon representation of the scarcity of
certain things. And some resources are finite, so I am not so extreme as to advocate that we get rid
of the concept altogether. But education, healthcare, and other things that can be seen as services
are not finite. There is enough housing for everyone, or at the very least enough material to make
enough housing for everyone. We live in a society of self-imposed scarcity, which psychologically
makes people enter survival states. Everyone wants to hoard as much as possible, so everybody feels
they need to grind and grift. And so much of our technology is built around selling us stuff that
we need to buy or be left behind. So instead of fighting against the grift, it feels like you have
to join it.

### "Democratizing knowledge"
Early on, when LLMs were becoming popular, people would talk about how they were democratizing
knowledge and skills, but that topic seemed to disappear pretty quickly once people realized
the goal of these AI companies is actually the opposite. They want to control knowledge and skills,
and make sure people have to pay to use them. Taking something inherent to all of us and turning
it into a finite resource to sell back to us. Putting our entire economy and social system in a
stranglehold without even asking if that is what we want.

But imagine a world where society worked how it should. Where people don't have to work three jobs
just to afford rent, and people don't have to sell their souls to companies just to pay their bills.
Researchers could research what they want without worrying about politicized grants. Authors could
write what they want without worrying about what sells, and artists would be free to make their art.
Imagine how fulfilling the world could be for all of us if we could get rid of the grind and nourish
the things that make us human.

We could have that if people spent even half the time trying to advance society as they do trying
to advance technology. To advance technology responsibly, we need a functioning system, which we
have never been further away from than we are today. So instead of trying to take advantage of a
system that wants to make things harder for everyone, why don't you try to change it? Don't just
accept things as inevitable, fight for something better and make it happen.
